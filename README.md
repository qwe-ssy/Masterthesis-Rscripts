# Health Communication Research: Processing of FOP Nutrition Labels & Interventions
Have you ever noticed how a bold "High in Protein" claim can instantly persuade a consumer, even when a glaring Nutri-Score 'E' warns otherwise?

This everyday collision between nutrition claims and nutri-score highlights a fundamental challenge in health communication. When facing conflicting signals, consumers often rely on **fast, heuristic processing**—falling prey to the "healthy halo" effect—which leaves them highly **vulnerable** to misleading cues. How do individuals process this cognitive imbalance? What individual factors (such as nutrition literacy) influence their vulnerability, and can brief intervention messages effectively trigger **systematic, reflective evaluation** to make healthier choices?

To answer these questions, this repository contains the complete quantitative analysis pipeline and reproducible R scripts for my Master of Science thesis: **"The Effect of Front-of-Pack Nutrition Label Congruency on Purchase Intentions: The Moderating Roles of Nutrition Literacy and a Nutri-Score Intervention Message"** at the University of Amsterdam. Grounded in dual-process models of reasoning (specifically the Heuristic-Systematic Model), this research provides a mechanism-testing perspective on how consumers navigate contradictory health information

## Experimental Design
A 2 (label congruency: incongruent vs. congruent) × 2 (nutrition literacy: low vs. high) × 2 (Nutri-Score intervention message: absent vs. present) between-subjects online experiment was conducted among 220 Dutch consumers, using an energy bar as the product stimulus. ## Key findings & Visualizations
Based on the 2x2x2 between-subjects online experiment (N=220), the quantitative analysis yielded three key insights:

*   **The Main Effect of Label Congruency:** Products displaying congruent label combinations (healthy claim + Nutri-Score A/B) led to significantly stronger purchase intentions than those with incongruent combinations (healthy claim + Nutri-Score D/E) ($p < .001$). As discussed in the research, the unhealthy Nutri-Score effectively acted as a powerful **"avoidance signal"** that successfully counteracted the "healthy halo" effect generated by the marketing claims.
*   **The Absence of Moderating Effects:** Interestingly, neither consumers' nutrition literacy nor the presence of a Nutri-Score intervention message significantly moderated the relation between label congruency and purchase intentions ($p > .05$). **As visualized in the interaction plot below**, the lower purchase intentions for incongruent products remained remarkably consistent across all experimental conditions.
*   **Policy & Industry Implications:** These findings highlight the critical necessity of **alignment** between marketing-driven nutrition claims and objective Nutri-Score ratings. Furthermore, the lack of moderation effects suggests that simple explanatory messages are insufficient to change consumer behavior, emphasizing the need for more sustainable intervention strategies to reduce consumer confusion.
